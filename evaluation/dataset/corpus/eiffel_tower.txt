The Eiffel Tower

The Eiffel Tower is a wrought-iron lattice tower located on the Champ de Mars
in Paris, France. It is named after the engineer Gustave Eiffel, whose company
designed and built the tower. Construction began in 1887 and was completed in
1889, in time for the 1889 World's Fair, which celebrated the centennial of
the French Revolution.

The tower stands 330 meters (1,083 feet) tall, which is roughly the same
height as an 81-storey building. When it was completed, it was the tallest
man-made structure in the world, a title it held for 41 years, until the
Chrysler Building in New York City was finished in 1930.

The Eiffel Tower has three levels accessible to visitors. Restaurants are
located on the first and second levels. The top level's upper platform is
276 meters above the ground, making it the highest observation deck
accessible to the public in the European Union.

The tower was initially criticized by some of France's leading artists and
intellectuals for its design, but it has become a global cultural icon of
France and one of the most recognizable structures in the world. It is also
one of the most visited paid monuments in the world, with millions of
visitors ascending it every year.

The tower is repainted every seven years to protect it from rust. Painting
the tower requires around 60 tonnes of paint. Maintenance of this kind is a
significant and recurring part of preserving the structure.

Gustave Eiffel used his apartment at the top of the tower to conduct
scientific experiments, including experiments related to meteorology and
radio transmission, since the tower's height made it useful as an antenna.
